REFERENCE [1]

You Only Look Once: Unified, Real-Time Object Detection

Author
Joseph Redmon, Santosh Divvala, Ross Girshick, and Ali Farhadi
Date
2016; preprint first submitted June 8, 2015
Source
CVPR 2016; arXiv author manuscript

Synopsis

Presents a unified detector that predicts bounding boxes and class probabilities from an image. This is historical context for locating name regions; the project uses a later Ultralytics implementation.

Reliability rating

High for the method: original research published at CVPR. Its experiments do not establish performance for this Android app.

REFERENCE [4]

Character Region Awareness for Text Detection

Author
Youngmin Baek, Bado Lee, Dongyoon Han, Sangdoo Yun, and Hwalsuk Lee
Date
2019
Source
Proceedings of CVPR 2019; CVF Open Access

Open author manuscript ↗

Synopsis

CRAFT predicts character regions and connections to locate scene text. It offers a text-focused alternative to product-name detection, but selecting the identifying name remains a separate decision. CRAFT was not evaluated in this project.

Reliability rating

High for the algorithm description: a peer-reviewed paper from its authors. Published benchmarks do not establish superiority on these packaging photos or the target phone.

REFERENCE [5]

An End-to-End Trainable Neural Network for Image-Based Sequence Recognition and Its Application to Scene Text Recognition

Author
Baoguang Shi, Xiang Bai, and Cong Yao
Date
2017; author preprint July 21, 2015
Source
IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence, volume 39, issue 11

Open author manuscript ↗

Synopsis

Combines image-feature extraction, sequence modeling, and transcription in a trainable recognition system. It illustrates reading a text image, distinct from locating text in a full scene. It is background rather than a system evaluated here.

Reliability rating

High for the method: original peer-reviewed journal research with an accessible author manuscript. Recognition benchmarks cannot substitute for full app evaluation.

REFERENCE [6]

Seeing AI App is Now Available in the iOS app store

Author
Microsoft Accessibility Blog
Date
July 12, 2017
Source
Microsoft Accessibility Blog

Synopsis

The launch announcement describes an accessibility app that speaks visual information. Short-text reading and barcode-based product identification provide context for comparing direct OCR with product lookup. This is a historical example.

Reliability rating

High for the features announced at launch; moderate as evidence of effectiveness. It is a first-party announcement rather than an independent usability study or comparative benchmark.
